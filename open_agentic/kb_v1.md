### Anthracnose
Irregular dark brown to black lesions on stems, pods, and petioles with dark acervuli (pin-cushion-like structures) visible on infected tissue. Unlike White_Mold, Anthracnose produces NO white fluffy mycelium and no large black sclerotia — instead look for tiny dark spines (setae) visible with a hand lens. Unlike Diaporthe (parallel linear rows of pycnidia on dry mature stems), Anthracnose lesions are irregular in shape and distribution, and may show dark mycelium filling pod cavities. Infected stems develop dark (not bleached) lesions; brown veins and curling leaves accompany severe infections.

### Bacterial_Blight
Angular, vein-bounded lesions with dark reddish-brown necrotic centers surrounded by prominent yellowish-green halos on young, upper-canopy leaves. Unlike Frogeye_leaf_spot (circular spots with reddish-purple ring borders, no yellow-green halos), Bacterial_Blight lesions are angular (constrained by veins) and always have a distinct yellow-green halo. Unlike Phyllosticta (gray/tan lesions, narrow dark margins), Bacterial_Blight centers are dark reddish-brown with conspicuous halos. Lesions coalesce into large irregular necrotic areas causing ragged foliage; most common on young leaves in mid-to-upper canopy.

### Bacterial_Pustule
Small, light green spots developing into light-colored tan/cream RAISED pustules (blisters), most visible on leaf undersides, with no dark centers and no yellow-green halos. Unlike Bacterial_Blight (dark reddish-brown centers with prominent yellow-green halos), pustules are LIGHT-colored and lack halos entirely. Unlike Soybean rust (dark reddish-brown spots with visible pore openings and orange spore masses on undersides), Bacterial_Pustule produces tan bumps with no openings or spore masses — any crack on the pustule is a linear slit, not a pore. Unlike Septoria (angular dark red-brown spots embedded in leaf tissue), pustules are raised above the leaf surface and pale-colored.

### Bean_Pod_Mottle_virus
Diffuse chlorotic mottling or mosaic pattern of irregular yellow-green patches on leaves — a blotchy discoloration without discrete defined lesion edges. Unlike Frogeye (discrete circular spots with reddish-purple borders), Powdery_Mildew (white powdery surface coating), or Anthracnose (dark pod lesions), BPMV shows no fungal structures and no discrete spot edges — only irregular light/dark green color variation across the leaf. Severe infections add leaf puckering, distortion, and stunting. May cause green stem syndrome (stems remain green after pod maturity). No pathogen signs visible; cool temperatures favor symptom expression.

### Brown_Stem_Rot
CRITICAL diagnostic: split the lower stem — the pith turns BROWN, which distinguishes BSR from Sudden Death Syndrome (white pith) and Fusarium (brown vascular tissue but pith may vary). Foliar symptoms — interveinal chlorosis and necrosis (yellowing and browning between veins while veins stay green), followed by wilting — are nearly identical to SDS; the brown pith is the only reliable field distinction. Unlike Bean_Pod_Mottle_virus (diffuse mottling without interveinal necrosis pattern), BSR shows clear progression from interveinal yellowing to crisp brown necrotic areas.

### Cercospora
Diffuse purple-bronze to reddish-purple DISCOLORATION of leaf surfaces exposed to light — leaves appear "sunburned" and become leathery, not spotty. Unlike Septoria_brown_spot (discrete angular red-brown SPOTS with defined edges scattered on older leaves), Cercospora shows broad purple-bronze COLOR CHANGES across leaf surfaces without discrete spot margins. Unlike Bacterial_Blight (angular spots with prominent yellow-green halos), Cercospora discoloration has no halos. Small dark spots may coalesce along major veins; reddish-purple lesions also appear on petioles, stems, and pods; pink to purple discoloration on seed coats.

### Purple_Seed_Stain
Pink to purple to dark irregular discoloration specifically on soybean SEED COATS; pods show no distinctive external symptoms. Unlike Frogeye_leaf_spot (which affects leaves with circular gray-centered spots), Purple_Seed_Stain is a SEED symptom only. Caused by Cercospora kikuchii; staining varies from faint pink to near-black blotches spreading irregularly across seed coats.

### Charcoal_Rot
Stems and roots near the soil line appear GRAY with tiny black specks resembling charcoal dust — these are microsclerotia embedded in vascular and pith tissue. Unlike Phytophthora (chocolate-BROWN stem discoloration, no gray color, no tiny specks), Charcoal_Rot shows a distinctly GRAY stem with scattered black dot microsclerotia. Unlike SDS (white pith, blue fungal growth on roots), Charcoal_Rot has reddish-brown pith with gray hyphae giving a marbled cross-section. Cross-sections of lower stem show gray streaking; plants wilt and die under drought stress; tiny black pycnidia on outer stem surface.

### Frogeye_leaf_spot
Small to medium CIRCULAR spots (1–5 mm) with grayish-tan centers and a distinct reddish-brown to purple RING border; yellow halo may surround spots. Unlike Bacterial_Blight (angular vein-bounded lesions with yellow-green halos, no circular shape), Frogeye spots are ROUND with a colored border ring, not angular. Unlike Phyllosticta (gray/tan irregular or V-shaped lesions), Frogeye has a clear reddish-purple ring. Centers may have small black dots or fall away leaving shot holes. Frogeye affects LEAVES — if viewing seeds or pods, consider Purple_Seed_Stain or Phomopsis instead.

### Diaporthe
Parallel LINEAR ROWS of tiny black flask-shaped pycnidia arranged along DRY, MATURE soybean stems — appears only late season (R6–R8). Unlike White_Mold (white fluffy mycelium with large oval black sclerotia, bleached stems), Diaporthe has NO white mycelium and NO large sclerotia — only tiny ordered black dots on stems. Unlike Brown_Stem_Rot (interveinal leaf chlorosis, brown pith), Diaporthe's primary sign is stem pycnidia on late-season dry stems without prominent foliar symptoms. Unlike Phytophthora (chocolate-brown stem from soil), Diaporthe pycnidia are surface structures on already-dead/mature stems.

### Phomopsis
Infected seeds are cracked, shriveled, dull, and chalky — they may have a thin GRAY (not white fluffy) mold. Unlike White_Mold (WHITE cottony mycelium filling pod cavities with large embedded black sclerotia), Phomopsis produces NO white fluffy mycelial growth inside pods. Pod infection may show circular ring-pattern pycnidia on the exterior pod surface. Seeds from infected pods decompose post-harvest; seedlings from infected seeds are often blighted.

### Phyllosticta_leaf_spot
Lesions are circular, oval, irregular, or V-shaped with GRAY or TAN centers and a narrow, dark (not reddish-purple) margin — often on lower canopy leaves. Unlike Bacterial_Blight (dark reddish-brown centers with prominent YELLOW-GREEN halos, angular shape), Phyllosticta centers are gray/tan with only a thin dark border and no halos. Unlike Frogeye (circular spots with a distinct reddish-purple ring), Phyllosticta has a narrow dark margin without a thick colored ring and shapes are often irregular or V-shaped. Older lesions may have small black pycnidia (specks) in the center.

### Phytophthora
Diagnostic: CHOCOLATE-BROWN stem discoloration beginning BELOW THE SOIL LINE and extending upward several nodes — the color is distinctly dark brown (not gray). Unlike Charcoal_Rot (GRAY stems with tiny charcoal-dust microsclerotia), Phytophthora shows solid chocolate-brown discoloration, not gray with specks. Unlike Stem_Canker (dark sunken cankers at nodes, no soil-line origin), Phytophthora discoloration originates at soil level and moves upward. Leaves turn yellow then wilt and die but REMAIN ATTACHED to stems. Roots show brown surface with brown inner tissue; lateral roots nearly completely discolored.

### Powdery_Mildew
White to gray POWDERY COATING on leaf surfaces that coalesces to cover entire leaves — the powdery layer sits ON the leaf surface and can be wiped. Unlike Septoria_brown_spot (angular reddish-brown SPOTS embedded within leaf tissue, no surface coating), Powdery_Mildew shows a superficial white powder, not spots. Unlike Downy_mildew (pale yellow spots on upper surface with sporulation on lower surface), Powdery_Mildew has visible white coating on UPPER leaf surfaces. Pods may be infected, leading to shriveled seeds; some varieties show leaf yellowing or rust-colored patches beneath the powdery growth.

### Stem_Canker
Elongated, sunken, DARK BROWN cankers on stems, typically originating at nodes or petiole bases — the stem lesions are DARK (not bleached). Unlike White_Mold (WHITE fluffy mycelium, large black oval sclerotia, BLEACHED pale stems), Stem_Canker has NO white mycelium and NO large sclerotia — only dark sunken tissue with tiny black perithecia. The cankers are dark brown and sunken, not pale/bleached. Interveinal chlorosis and necrosis may develop in leaves above the canker. Stem cankers do not affect roots (unlike Phytophthora).

### Green_stem_disorder
Stems and leaves remain distinctly green and moist while pods and seeds have fully ripened and turned brown — and while neighboring plants have matured normally. No lesions, spots, fungal structures, or interveinal necrosis are present; the plant simply fails to senesce. Unlike Tobacco_Streak_Virus (which also shows green stems but with necrotic streaks at nodes and dead/distorted leaves), Green_stem_disorder shows clean green tissue with no necrosis. Unlike BPMV (mosaic/mottling on leaves), Green_stem_disorder leaves are uniformly green without mottling.

### Sudden_death_syndrome
CRITICAL diagnostic: split the lower stem — the PITH REMAINS WHITE/CREAM, distinguishing SDS from Brown_Stem_Rot (brown pith). Foliar symptoms are diffuse interveinal chlorosis — scattered yellow spots between veins that expand to brown necrotic areas — on multiple leaves simultaneously, with leaves often cupped or curled. Unlike Bacterial_Blight (discrete angular spots with yellow-green halos), SDS shows DIFFUSE interveinal yellowing across the whole leaf without discrete halo-bordered spots. In moist conditions, blue fungal growth may appear on ROOTS (not stems). Brown-grey vascular discoloration in lower stem; pith stays white.

### Fusarium
Reddish-brown to dark brown discolored roots with brown VASCULAR TISSUE visible in cross-sections of roots and lower stem — vascular discoloration, not pith discoloration. Unlike SDS (WHITE pith, blue root fungal growth), Fusarium shows BROWN vascular tissue throughout roots and stems with no blue growth. Unlike White_Mold (white fluffy mycelium, large black sclerotia), Fusarium has NO external white mycelial growth. Unlike Green_stem_disorder (no lesions, no root rot), Fusarium shows clear root decay and brown vascular tissue. Foliar symptoms: interveinal chlorosis, scorching, wilting.

### Pythium_damping_off
Seedling-stage disease: infected seeds appear rotted with soil sticking to them; seedlings develop water-soaked lesions on hypocotyl/cotyledons that turn into brown SOFT ROT — rotted roots cause plants to pull easily from soil. Unlike Rhizoctonia (which causes a distinct reddish-orange to brown FIRM stem canker at the soil line on slightly older seedlings), Pythium causes soft, water-soaked collapse. Unlike Phytophthora (brown discoloration on older plants), Pythium causes pre- and early post-emergence soft rot on very young seedlings. Symptomatic tissue ranges from light tan to near-black.

### Septoria_brown_spot
Angular, RED-TO-BROWN spots of varying sizes (tiny specks to ~6 mm) on LOWER CANOPY leaves first, progressing upward; affected leaves rapidly turn YELLOW and drop, causing bottom-up defoliation. Unlike Bacterial_Blight (fewer isolated spots with prominent YELLOW-GREEN HALOS on YOUNG UPPER canopy leaves), Septoria produces dense small spots densely scattered on OLDER LOWER leaves without distinct halos. Unlike Downy_mildew (pale yellow spots, not angular or red-brown), Septoria spots are angular and distinctly reddish-brown. Tiny raised pycnidia visible with hand lens in brown areas; surrounding tissue yellows before leaf drops.

### White_Mold
Definitive signs: WHITE, FLUFFY cottony mycelium on stems (especially at nodes) plus LARGE, HARD, BLACK OVAL sclerotia (the size of rat droppings) embedded in and on stems. Unlike Stem_Canker (dark sunken cankers, NO white growth, NO large sclerotia), White_Mold always has visible white mycelial growth. Unlike Phytophthora (no white growth, chocolate-brown stem from soil), White_Mold has white fluffy mycelium at node lesions and bleached dead stems. Unlike Diaporthe (tiny ordered black dots in rows), White_Mold has large irregularly-placed black sclerotia. Dead leaves remain firmly attached to bleached-white stems; white cottony growth may also be found inside pods.

### Top_Dieback
Bright yellowing and MARGINAL NECROSIS specifically on UPPER/NEWEST canopy leaves in early-to-mid August, progressing to dead tissue along leaf edges that falls out leaving a ragged appearance. Unlike SDS (interveinal chlorosis across entire leaf blades on multiple canopy levels), Top_Dieback shows MARGINAL yellowing/browning concentrated on the UPPERMOST new leaves. Unlike Potassium deficiency (similar marginal chlorosis but uniform across the canopy), Top_Dieback follows row patterns or specific field areas. Small, shriveled seeds at maturity; symptoms begin at outer leaf margins of the topmost leaves.

### Tobacco_Streak_Virus
Necrotic DEAD STREAKS and discoloration at NODES, bud blight (growing tip dies), proliferation of small leaves/buds, and dark spots on pods — combined with overall stunting and wilting. Unlike Green_stem_disorder (clean green stems with no necrosis, no dead tissue), TSV shows necrotic streaks and dead areas AT NODES and at buds. Unlike Bean_Pod_Mottle_virus (mosaic/mottling on leaves, green stem, NO necrotic node streaks), TSV has distinct NECROSIS at nodes and dead/distorted buds. Unlike White_Mold (white fluffy mycelium), TSV shows no fungal growth. Pod dark spots and delayed/discolored seed development are characteristic.
