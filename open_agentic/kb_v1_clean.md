### Anthracnose
Brown, irregularly shaped spots on stems, pods, and petioles with dark **acervuli (pin cushion-like structures)** developing in lesions — these are visible at 20X magnification with tiny black spines (setae). Unlike Diaporthe (which shows **parallel linear rows** of flask-shaped pycnidia on mature dry stems), Anthracnose acervuli are scattered and pin-cushion shaped, not linearly arranged. Infected pods show irregular dark blotches with acervuli; pod blanking where mycelium fills pod cavity.

### Bacterial_Blight
Lesions are **angular** (following leaf veins), initially water-soaked, turning yellow then brown — surrounded by a **yellowish-green halo** (not a purple ring). Unlike Frogeye leaf spot (circular spots with reddish-**purple** border and shot-hole centers), Bacterial_Blight spots are angular and water-soaked. Most common on **young upper canopy leaves**; lesions may coalesce into large ragged necrotic areas with leaf veins turning necrotic in a lightning-bolt pattern.

### Brown_Stem_Rot
Brown discoloration of the stem pith, especially at and between nodes near the soil line; foliar symptoms include interveinal chlorosis and necrosis (yellowing and browning of tissue between leaf veins), followed by leaf wilting and curling.. Brown discoloration of the pith (internal stem tissue), best observed at full pod stage; the pith remains brown unlike in sudden death syndrome where it stays white or cream-colored.. Sudden Death Syndrome

### Bean_Pod_Mottle_virus
Primary diagnostic feature is **foliar mosaic and mottling** — chlorotic mottling on leaves ranging from mild to severe mosaic with possible leaf distortion, stunting, and puckering. Unlike Anthracnose (dark irregular blotches on pods with pin-cushion acervuli) and Purple_Seed_Stain (uniform pink-purple seed coat staining), BPMV's main symptom is irregular leaf mosaic. Seed discoloration and green stem syndrome may also occur; no physical signs of pathogen — confirmation requires lab testing.

### Cercospora
The first symptom is light purple spots on the top surface of leaves that expand to reddish-purple or bronze, giving a **"sunburned" leathery appearance** — caused by cercosporin toxin in light. This distinct purple-bronze leaf discoloration is unlike Phomopsis (which shows cracked, shriveled, dull seeds without leaf purpling). Small dark spots develop along major leaf veins; reddish-purple lesions on petioles, stems, and pods; pink to purple discoloration on seed coats.

### Purple_Seed_Stain
Pink to purple discoloration on seed coats of infected seeds; soybean pods infected by C. kikuchii develop purple seed stain.. Pink to purple discoloration specifically on seed coats

### Bacterial_Pustule
Small, light green spots with **raised centers (pustules/blisters)** — especially on leaf **undersides** — that develop into light-colored tan bumps without spore masses. Unlike Septoria brown spot (angular red-brown spots with progressive defoliation from lower canopy upward and general chlorosis), Bacterial_Pustule shows raised tan bumps on undersides with less widespread chlorosis and minimal defoliation. Lesions can coalesce into large irregular brown areas; pustule openings are typically a linear crack across the surface, unlike soybean rust.

### Charcoal_Rot
Roots and lower stems appear **gray with tiny black specks resembling charcoal dust** on or just inside the tissue surface — the charcoal-like microsclerotia are diagnostic. Unlike Phytophthora (chocolate-brown stem discoloration from soil line upward with no charcoal specks) and unlike SDS (white pith with blue root fungal growth), Charcoal_Rot shows a **gray** stem with marbled cross-section and charcoal-dust specks. Plants may be stunted and wilted with small chlorotic leaves that remain attached.

### Frogeye_leaf_spot
Early symptoms are small, dark, water-soaked circular to angular lesions (generally 1-5 mm diameter) that expand into brown to grayish spots with brown-reddish borders. A yellow halo may be present around leaf spots. Centers of spots may show small black dots (spores) or fall away leaving a 'shot hole' appearance.. The reddish-brown or purple ring surrounding round leaf spots distinguishes frogeye leaf spot. Small black dots resembling fine ground pepper (fungal spores) visible in the middle of lesions.

### Diaporthe
**Parallel linear rows** of black, flask-shaped pycnidia on mature soybean stems occurring late season (R6–R8). This linear arrangement of pycnidia on stems is diagnostic — unlike Brown_Stem_Rot (foliar interveinal chlorosis with brown pith, no surface pycnidia) and unlike Phytophthora (chocolate-brown lower stem discoloration from soil upward). Diaporthe shows no interveinal chlorosis; the external stem surface has discrete black dot rows while the plant may otherwise appear normal or dry.

### Phomopsis
Infected seeds are **cracked, shriveled, dull**, and may have a **gray mold** — without the fluffy white cottony mycelium seen in White Mold. Unlike White Mold (which shows obvious fluffy white mycelium and large embedded black sclerotia in pods), Phomopsis shows dull grayish deteriorated seeds without cottony growth. Seedlings grown from infected seeds may be blighted; infected seed decompose after harvest with low viability.

### Phyllosticta_leaf_spot
Lesions are circular, oval, irregular, or V-shaped with a **gray or tan center** and narrow dark margin — found predominantly in the **lower canopy**. Unlike Bacterial_Blight (angular water-soaked lesions with yellow-green halos on upper canopy), Phyllosticta lesions are dry, gray-tan, without water-soaked halos, and occur lower in the canopy. In older lesions, numerous small black pycnidia are visible within the lesion center.

### Phytophthora
The diagnostic feature is **dark chocolate-brown stem discoloration** extending from below the soil line upward several nodes, with lateral and branch roots almost completely discolored. Unlike Soybean Cyst Nematode (stunted plants in field patches with root cysts but **no stem discoloration**), Phytophthora shows clear chocolate-brown lower stem coloration. Leaves yellow and wilt but typically **stay attached** after plant death; mature plants show brown discolored roots with general yellowing progressing upward.. stem canker, Pythium

### Powdery_Mildew
White to gray powdery patches on leaves that begin small but coalesce to cover the entire leaf as disease progresses. Some varieties exhibit leaf yellowing, scorching, or rust-colored patch symptoms. Pods may become heavily infected resulting in shriveled, underdeveloped seed.. White, powdery coating on infected leaves that coalesces to cover the entire leaf; necrotic tissues may mimic herbicide injury symptoms.. herbicide injuries

### Stem_Canker
Early symptoms are reddish-brown lesions at the base of branches or leaf petioles that develop into elongated, sunken, dark brown cankers spreading up and down the stem. Tiny black dots called perithecia may appear on the stem singly or in clustered groups on plants killed by stem canker. Leaves may develop necrosis and chlorosis between the veins.. Elongated, sunken, dark brown cankers on stems with tiny black perithecia. Reddish-brown discoloration may also occur inside the stem. Lesions often develop at and remain darker at nodes. Stem cankers do not impact the root, unlike Phytophthora root rot.. Phytophthora root rot, Southern stem canker

### Green_stem_disorder
All or part of the soybean plant remains green while the rest of the plant or neighboring plants mature, most commonly seen in stems, leaves or pods; stems remain moist while pods and seeds have ripened.. Stems and occasionally leaves remain green and moist after pods and seeds have ripened and turned brown; excessive leaves on plant and green stems when neighboring plants are near harvest maturity.

### Sudden_death_syndrome
Foliar symptoms begin as scattered yellow diffuse spots between veins expanding to brown lesions with chlorotic halos; leaves may be cupped or curled. **The pith remains WHITE** — this is the key differential from Brown Stem Rot (brown pith) and Charcoal Rot (gray pith with charcoal specks). Both SDS and Brown Stem Rot show interveinal chlorosis, but splitting the stem reveals white pith in SDS; roots may show blue fungal growth in moist conditions.. Brown stem rot

### Fusarium
Root rot appears as reddish-brown to dark brown **vascular discoloration** in roots and lower stems, with cortical decay and poor nodulation. Unlike SDS (which also shows vascular discoloration but with **white pith** intact), Fusarium shows general root cortical decay; if you see **white pith** in the stem, it is SDS not Fusarium. Unlike White Mold (which shows large obvious black sclerotia embedded in stem), Fusarium has no large sclerotia — only vascular discoloration and foliar interveinal chlorosis.. Pythium, Phytophthora, Rhizoctonia

### Pythium_damping_off
Infected seeds appear rotted with soil sticking to them, and infected seedlings develop water-soaked lesions on the hypocotyl or cotyledons that turn into brown soft rot, with rotted roots that allow diseased plants to be easily pulled from soil. Symptomatic areas of plants can range in color from light tan to almost black.. Water-soaked lesions developing into brown soft rot on seedlings, with rotted roots that cause plants to pull easily from soil, and rotted seeds with soil sticking to them.. Phytophthora, Fusarium, Rhizoctonia

### Rhizoctonia
Reddish-brown, firm, **dry** sunken canker on the hypocotyl or lower stem of seedlings, often with a distinct reddish-brown border. Unlike Pythium damping-off (water-soaked **soft rot** with mushy texture and soil sticking to roots), Rhizoctonia lesions are **dry and firm** — not mushy — with a clearly defined reddish-brown discoloration. Seedlings may collapse but the lesion margin is sharp and dry, not water-soaked.

### Septoria_brown_spot
**Angular**, red to brown spots varying from tiny specks to quarter-inch diameter on upper and lower leaf surfaces, with defoliation progressing from **bottom of plant upward**. Unlike Frogeye leaf spot (circular spots with a distinct **reddish-purple ring/border**), Septoria spots are angular with no purple ring border. Unlike Soybean rust (raised pustules on leaf undersides), Septoria shows flat angular lesions; pycnidia (tiny raised specks) may be visible with a hand lens in lesion centers.. soybean rust, Bacterial blight

### Soybean_Cyst_Nematode
Stunted, yellowed plants in **field patches or row-aligned bands** with poor root development; diagnostic feature is **tiny white or yellow lemon-shaped cysts** visible on roots. Unlike Brown Stem Rot and SDS (which show interveinal chlorosis with internal stem damage), SCN diagnosis requires examining roots for cysts — no stem discoloration or internal pith changes occur. Field patterns show irregular patches of reduced stand; symptoms worsen in susceptible variety rows adjacent to tolerant rows.

### Soybean_Dwarf_Mosaic_Virus
Irregular **mosaic and mottling** across foliage with leaf rugosity, distortion, and plant stunting; no fungal structures present. Unlike White Mold (white cottony mycelium and black sclerotia on stems), SDMV is a virus with no visible fungal growth — only irregular leaf coloration and distortion. Unlike Downy mildew (discrete yellow spots with fuzzy underside sporulation), SDMV shows mosaic coloration distributed irregularly across leaves with possible plant stunting.

### Soybean_Vein_necrosis_virus
Necrosis specifically **following vein patterns** — dark brown to black discoloration along veins with surrounding chlorosis, creating a vein-blackening symptom. Unlike Frogeye leaf spot (discrete **circular** spots with a purple ring, not vein-following) and unlike Bacterial_Blight (angular water-soaked lesions with yellow-green halos), SVNV necrosis traces along veins rather than forming discrete spot lesions. Affected leaves show the vein necrosis pattern distinctly; no fungal structures present.

### Soybean_rust
Tan to brown **angular lesions** on upper leaf surface with small raised pustules (uredinia) producing spores on **leaf undersides** — examining the underside is diagnostic. Unlike Powdery Mildew (white powdery coating on the **upper** leaf surface), rust pustules are on undersides and are tan-brown not white. Unlike Septoria (flat angular brown lesions with no raised structures), rust uredinia are raised with a visible pore opening on leaf undersides.

### White_Mold
White mold is recognized by **fluffy white cottony mycelium** on stems at nodes with **large black sclerotia** embedded within or inside stems — both features are diagnostic. Unlike Phytophthora (which causes dead plants with leaves attached but **no white mycelium or sclerotia**), White Mold always shows these fungal structures at nodes. Dead leaves remain attached and stems bleach white; absence of white mycelium and black sclerotia rules out White Mold.. Bird's nest fungi

### Top_Dieback
Initial symptoms appear as bright yellowing and tissue death on outer leaf margins of upper canopy leaves in early to mid August, followed by discoloration at nodes, brown edges, ragged appearance as dead tissue falls out, and eventual whole leaf death with small shriveled seeds at maturity. The uppermost leaves turn yellow/brown along the leaf margins.. Symptoms occur on upper/new plant leaves rather than lower leaves, and affected plants often follow row patterns or specific field areas. Unlike potassium deficiency, yellowing and browning is along leaf margins of the uppermost new leaves.. Potassium Deficiency, Sudden Death Syndrome

### Tobacco_Streak_Virus
Symptoms include bud blight, shepherd's crooking, stunting, and **dead/necrotic streaks at nodes** — without any white fluffy mycelium. Unlike White Mold (which shows white cottony mycelium and large black sclerotia at nodes), TSV is a virus with **no fungal structures** — nodes show only dark discoloration and necrotic streaks, not cottony growth. Additional features: wilting, leaf proliferation, dark spots on pods, discolored distorted leaves, and delayed seed development.
